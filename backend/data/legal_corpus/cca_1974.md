# Consumer Credit Act 1974 & the Limitation Act 1980

Governs the formal requirements for regulated consumer credit debts
(credit cards, loans, catalogue/store credit, overdrafts in some cases) and
when they become legally unenforceable through age.

## Key mechanics

- **Sections 77/78 - information requests ("CCA requests")**: a debtor can
  formally request a copy of the executed credit agreement; if the
  creditor cannot produce it within the statutory period, the debt becomes
  temporarily unenforceable (though it does not disappear) until they do.
- **Section 87 - default notices**: before a creditor can demand early
  repayment, terminate the agreement, or start enforcement action, they
  must serve a compliant default notice giving the debtor a chance to
  remedy the breach; a defective or missing default notice is a procedural
  ground to challenge enforcement.
- **Limitation Act 1980, s.5 - 6-year limitation on simple contract debts**:
  if a creditor has not obtained a judgment or had a written acknowledgment/
  payment from the debtor within 6 years of the last acknowledgment or
  payment, the debt becomes "statute-barred" - still technically owed, but
  no longer enforceable through the courts. This does not apply to some
  debt types (e.g. most tax debts, some secured debts).

## How Breathing Room uses this

Used to check, from the debt's age and payment history as described by the
user, whether a CCA information request or a statute-barred point is worth
raising before any Standard Financial Statement payment plan is agreed -
there is no point budgeting to pay a debt that may not be legally
enforceable at all.
