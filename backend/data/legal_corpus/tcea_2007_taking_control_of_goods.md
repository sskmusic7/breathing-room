# Taking Control of Goods - TCEA 2007 Sch.12 & the 2013/2014 Regulations

The modern statutory framework governing enforcement agents ("bailiffs"):
what notice they must give, how they must conduct themselves, what they can
and cannot take, and what fees they can charge at each stage.

## Key figures and rules

- **Fixed statutory fee stages** (Taking Control of Goods (Fees)
  Regulations 2014): a compliance stage fee (a fixed amount, commonly cited
  around £75), an enforcement stage fee once the agent visits (commonly
  cited around £235, plus a percentage above £1,500 owed), and a further
  sale-stage fee if goods are removed and sold. Fees must follow this
  structure exactly - extra/unexplained charges are challengeable.
- **Regulation 10 / vulnerability guidance**: enforcement agents must
  consider whether the debtor (or someone in the household) is in a
  "vulnerable situation" - including severe mental health conditions,
  serious illness, a single parent with young children present, or
  significant language/learning difficulties - and may need to withdraw or
  take control action more cautiously, or not remove goods, in such cases.
- **Notice of enforcement**: a minimum notice period (commonly 7 clear
  days, excluding Sundays/bank holidays) must be given before the first
  visit, in a prescribed form.
- **Restrictions**: agents cannot force entry for most civil debts on a
  first visit, cannot take goods belonging to a third party or "tools of
  the trade"/essential household items up to a value, and cannot attend
  certain days/times without specific authorisation.

## How Breathing Room uses this

Used at the "already escalated to enforcement" stage to check whether a fee
demanded is within the statutory schedule, whether proper notice was given,
and whether a vulnerability disclosure (Regulation 10) should be raised
directly with the enforcement agent/creditor to pause action.
