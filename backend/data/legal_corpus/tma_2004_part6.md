# Traffic Management Act 2004 - Part 6

Part 6 of the Traffic Management Act 2004 (TMA 2004) is the primary statutory
framework that lets local authorities in England and Wales enforce parking,
bus lane, and moving traffic contraventions themselves through civil
(non-criminal) Penalty Charge Notices (PCNs), rather than through the police
and criminal courts.

## Key mechanics for an appeal

- **Section 78**: a PCN issued by a civil enforcement officer must state the
  grounds of the contravention, the amount payable, the discount period, and
  how to make representations.
- **Statutory discount period**: councils commonly offer a 50% discount if
  the PCN is paid within 14 days of issue (the exact window is set by each
  authority's own scheme, but 14 days is standard practice under TMA 2004
  schemes).
- **Formal representations (28-day window)**: once a Notice to Owner (NTO) is
  served, the registered keeper has 28 days to make formal written
  representations. The council must respond in writing.
- **Statutory grounds for representation** (non-exhaustive, drawn from the
  Traffic Management Act 2004 (Civil Enforcement of Road Traffic
  Contraventions) framework and the Civil Enforcement of Parking
  Contraventions Representations and Appeals Regulations):
  - the alleged contravention did not occur;
  - the recipient was not the owner/keeper at the relevant time;
  - the vehicle was taken without consent;
  - the penalty charge exceeds the amount applicable;
  - procedural impropriety by the enforcement authority (e.g. defective
    notice, missed statutory deadline);
  - the vehicle was being used for police, fire, or ambulance purposes.
- **Procedural impropriety**: if the council fails to follow the statutory
  notice/timing requirements exactly, this is itself a valid ground to have
  the PCN cancelled, independent of the underlying facts of the alleged
  contravention.
- **Adjudication**: if the council rejects representations, the keeper can
  appeal to an independent tribunal (London Tribunals for London boroughs;
  the Traffic Penalty Tribunal for most of England and Wales outside London).

The RAG pipeline matches the extracted contravention code and notice stage
(initial PCN / Notice to Owner / Charge Certificate) against this framework to
identify (a) which statutory deadline currently applies and how many days are
left, and (b) which of the statutory grounds for representation the user's
described circumstances might support, before drafting a representation letter
for the user to review, sign, and submit themselves.
