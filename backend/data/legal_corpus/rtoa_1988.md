# Road Traffic Offenders Act 1988

Unlike council-issued PCNs, speeding and similar criminal road traffic
offences are prosecuted under this Act, and a Notice of Intended Prosecution
(NIP) is a criminal-process document, not a civil penalty notice.

## Key points

- **Section 1 - the 14-day rule**: a NIP must generally be served on the
  registered keeper within 14 days of the alleged offence, or the
  prosecution cannot normally proceed (subject to limited statutory
  exceptions, e.g. where the keeper's identity could not reasonably have
  been ascertained in time). A NIP served late is a strong statutory
  defence.
- **Section 172 - duty to identify the driver**: the registered keeper is
  legally required to name the driver at the time of the alleged offence
  within 28 days of the request, or commits a separate offence (points +
  fine) even if the original alleged offence is never proven.
- These are criminal proceedings, not civil PCNs - the Legal Services Act
  2007 "conduct of litigation"/reserved-activity boundary is especially
  important here: Breathing Room can only draft a response for the user to
  review and submit themselves (e.g. the s.172 driver-identification form),
  never submit or represent the user in the actual prosecution.
