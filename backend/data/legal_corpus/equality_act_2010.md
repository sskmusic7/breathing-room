# Equality Act 2010

The central UK statute protecting disabled people (a defined "protected
characteristic" including physical, mental, and neurodevelopmental
conditions such as ADHD and autism where they have a substantial, long-term
adverse effect on ability to carry out normal day-to-day activities) from
discrimination, and imposing positive duties on public bodies.

## Key provisions

- **Sections 20-21 - duty to make reasonable adjustments**: applies where a
  provision, criterion, or practice puts a disabled person at a substantial
  disadvantage compared to someone without the disability. For a local
  authority's parking enforcement, this can mean disregarding a rigid
  window in light of a documented crisis directly caused by the person's
  or a dependant's disability (e.g. a sudden sensory-overload episode
  requiring immediate, undelayable attention).
- **Section 149 - Public Sector Equality Duty (PSED)**: requires public
  authorities (including councils) to have "due regard" to the need to
  eliminate discrimination and advance equality of opportunity when
  exercising any of their functions, including parking enforcement
  decisions. A council that mechanically enforces a PCN without considering
  a properly raised disability/vulnerability point may be failing this
  duty.
- Neurodivergent conditions (ADHD, autism, etc.) are covered where they meet
  the Act's definition of disability; a diagnosis is not always required if
  the substantial, long-term adverse effect can otherwise be evidenced, but
  a formal diagnosis materially strengthens a representation.
