# Road Traffic Regulation Act 1984

The RTRA 1984 is the underlying statute that lets local authorities make
Traffic Regulation Orders (TROs) - the legal instruments that actually create
parking restrictions, yellow lines, bus lanes, and speed limits.

## Key points for an appeal

- **Section 14**: allows temporary/experimental traffic orders (e.g. for
  roadworks or trials). A restriction enforced without a valid, in-force TRO
  behind it is not lawfully enforceable - if the underlying order lapsed,
  was suspended, or was never properly made, that is a strong ground for
  cancellation.
- **Signage validity**: enforcement of a restriction depends on the signage
  actually matching the TRO and being clearly visible (obscured, faded, or
  missing signage undermines the legal basis for the PCN, per case law
  built on this framework, e.g. lines/signs not conforming to the Traffic
  Signs Regulations and General Directions).
- **Sections 88-89**: relate to speed limits and the orders that set them,
  relevant for cross-referencing Notices of Intended Prosecution for
  speeding offences.

## How Breathing Room uses this

Used to identify "signage/road-layout" grounds (e.g. yellow box junctions,
bus lane cameras at school-run times) where the physical/legal basis for the
restriction itself, not just the driver's conduct, is challengeable.
